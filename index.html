About

I'm completing a B.S. in Computer Science (Data Science concentration) and a B.A. in Immersive Media Design at the University of Maryland, graduating in May 2027.

Today's sensing and reconstruction systems optimize for pixel-level accuracy, but embodied interaction depends on what people actually notice and attend to. I want to build 3D reconstruction and rendering systems for XR and telepresence that adapt to the sensed state of the person using them and optimize for human perception, spending fidelity only where it matters.

That question came from the space between my two majors: a project I expected to be a straightforward reconstruction photobooth instead showed me where human and machine perception diverge. I plan to pursue a PhD to follow it fully, and to keep widening access to creative-technical work, through cheaper capture tools and by mentoring students entering the field.

    Deep learning for plankton estimation from satellite imagery

    Shipboard instruments measure plankton one point at a time, while NASA's PACE satellite observes the entire ocean surface. I proposed and led a project estimating plankton bloom biomass and community composition from hyperspectral satellite imagery and in-situ ocean data.

    The change I made
    The project began as visualization work on NASA's PACE data. Because I already had independent research experience, I was encouraged to branch off, and I redirected it toward deep learning: estimating plankton directly from the satellite imagery.
    My role
    Project lead and first author. I designed compact spatiotemporal CNN models for settings where paired satellite and in-situ examples are scarce, and grew the work from an independent study into a team project.
    Approach
    A grouped evaluation protocol that keeps near-duplicate observations out of both training and test sets.
    Timeline
    January to September 2026

    First-author manuscript currently under review.

    Embodied Dynamics Laboratory

    The EmD Lab engineers on-body interfaces that change how people perceive, learn, and share physical skills. I work on two of its human-subjects studies.

    My role
    I build study software and experimental conditions in Unity (C#) for Meta Quest 3, including a blur-based view-filtering effect grounded in visual-attention research. I also fabricate study hardware in Autodesk Fusion, set up and pilot-test studies, run participant sessions, and help annotate and analyze the data.
    Wearable perception study

    Embodied skill study
    A study in preparation on how wearable sensing (eye, head, and wrist tracking) captures hesitation during skilled physical tasks.
    Tools

    Representation learning for protein structure

    I study whether deep representations of protein structure generalize to protein families never seen during training, a central challenge for detecting distant evolutionary relationships between proteins.

    My role
    I developed hybrid deep learning architectures for protein structure representation, evaluated how well they generalize using statistical testing, and reviewed literature to guide the team's model design. I also started a new project from scratch applying the team's methods to a related problem.
    Approach
    Deep autoencoder models trained on protein structure data and evaluated on held-out protein families.
    Tools
    PyTorch, public protein structure databases, UMD high-performance computing
    Timeline
    September 2025 to October 2026

    Two manuscripts currently under review, both as co-first author.

  • Creative direction

    Project lead, with collaborator Abdul Rahem

    Memento of Self

    An interactive installation about the gap between a fluid, ever-changing self and its fixed digital capture. A multi-camera booth photographs each visitor, custom software reconstructs them as a painterly, dissolving 3D portrait, and they leave with a printed copy.

    The creative decision
    Early tests with four cameras failed both visually and geometrically. At seven, appearance held up near the input views while the underlying geometry dissolved and deformed. I chose to keep the seven-camera array and let that dissolve become the portrait itself, since it expressed the piece's idea of a self that can't be fixed in a single capture.
    Research question
    Does appearance-based (photometric) reconstruction degrade more gracefully than geometric accuracy when only a few camera views are available? An Azure Kinect depth camera provides independent geometric ground truth.
    My role
    I built the sparse-view Gaussian Splatting reconstruction pipeline, designed and fabricated the booth (housing, camera mounts, lighting, and trigger systems) with woodworking and physical computing, and integrated live rendering in Unreal Engine for exhibition.
    Finding
    Visitors responded positively to the portraits despite their poor geometry, and some older visitors said they reminded them of photos from the early 2000s. That gap between geometric accuracy and what people notice now drives my research on human-aligned reconstruction.
    Exhibitions
    New Works Incubator Showcase and Henson Mania: A Night of Art, Innovation & Imagination, University of Maryland
    Tools
    Gaussian Splatting, Azure Kinect, Unreal Engine, CNC fabrication, physical computing
    Timeline
    Summer 2026

    Independent final project, ARTT 370

    Into the Veins

    An interactive installation: stand in front of the screen, wave your hands, and two streams of glowing cells follow them through a pulsing, vein-like space.

    Hands
    1. Camera
    2. MediaPipe hand tracking
    3. Particle trails per hand
    Sound
    1. Score
    2. Audio amplitude
    3. Background motion
    Concept
    The piece imagines being inside a blood vessel. White cells with trailing tails drift through a red, vein-like space, and the viewer's hands become the current that steers them.
    The experience
    Each hand spawns its own stream of cells that follows it, so two hands create two separate trails. The background pulses with the amplitude of the instrumental of DPR IAN’s “SKINS.”
    What I built
    MediaPipe hand tracking driving an independent particle-trail system for each hand, plus an audio-reactive background driven by the score's amplitude.
    Exhibition
    On continuous display in the Immersive Media Design Department since July 2026.
    Tools
    TouchDesigner, MediaPipe hand tracking
    Timeline
    Spring 2026
